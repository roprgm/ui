A material is what a box is made of: its fill and its edge. A component is made of one, as a `Button` is of `material-control`, and the theme draws it. Never draw depth with a shadow or a border: pick the material.

Each material paints itself from its own variables, `--color-*` for its fill and `--shadow-*` for its edge. Set them again on any element, and every material inside it follows, without touching a component.

## Containers

They hold content, and are the background of what sits on them. In the default theme, the materials inside a container stand two levels over it, and a container on a card stands as a card on a card.

## Inside

Utilities, so variants such as `hover:` apply. Another fill, such as `bg-primary` or `bg-accent`, keeps the material's edge, as a primary `Button` does.

## A scope of your own

Any element that sets the variables again is a scope. A toolbar of glass lays its controls flat:

<Example name="materials/scope" />

```css
.toolbar-glass {
  background-color: oklch(20% 0 0 / 0.45);
  backdrop-filter: blur(12px);
  --color-control: oklch(100% 0 0 / 0.14);
  --color-control-hover: oklch(100% 0 0 / 0.22);
  --color-field: oklch(0% 0 0 / 0.18);
  --shadow-control: none;
  --shadow-field: none;
}
```

A container can't set its own material again for what's inside it, since it would change itself too: a card on a card takes a selector of its own, `:where(.material-card) .material-card`. A popup renders at the end of the page, outside every scope, so give its content `raised` over a card, or your scope's class.
